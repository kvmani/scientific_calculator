SCIENTIFIC GUIDE

Expression evaluation

The service parses an expression into a syntax tree, admits only numeric operations and approved functions, binds finite variables and an explicit angle convention, then evaluates one point or a bounded grid.

Algorithm

  1. Normalize ^ to exponentiation.
  2. Parse without executing Python source.
  3. Reject attributes, imports, keywords, and unapproved nodes.
  4. Bind constants and variables.
  5. Evaluate and reject complex or non-finite results.

Main equations

\[ x_{k} \;=\; x_{0} + k\,\Delta x, \qquad k = 0,1,\dots,N-1 \]

A plot evaluates the same validated expression on a bounded uniform grid.

\[ N \;=\; \left\lfloor \frac{x_{\text{stop}} - x_{0}}{\Delta x} \right\rfloor + 1 \]

The sample count sets plot resolution and is checked against the 5,000-point limit.

\[ \sin_{\deg}(\alpha) \;=\; \sin\!\left(\frac{\pi\,\alpha}{180}\right) \]

Degree mode converts trigonometric inputs before applying the standard radian functions.

where
\(x_{0}\)start of the plotted range
\(\Delta x\)positive step size
\(N\)number of sampled points, capped by the point limit
\(\alpha\)angle supplied in degrees

Critical inputs

Expression
Defines operators, functions, constants, and variables.
Variables
Must bind every non-constant name to a finite number.
Angle unit
Controls trigonometric inputs and inverse-trigonometric outputs.
Range and step
Control sampling density and must remain below 5,000 points.

Atom % ⇄ Mass %

The Atom ⇄ Mass % tab converts between atomic (mole) fraction and mass fraction for a composition of chemical elements, using IUPAC conventional standard atomic weights.

\[ w_{i} \;=\; \frac{x_{i}\,M_{i}}{\sum_{j} x_{j}\,M_{j}}, \qquad x_{i} \;=\; \frac{w_{i}/M_{i}}{\sum_{j} w_{j}/M_{j}} \]

Input values do not need to sum to 100; they are normalized before conversion. Results include each element's atomic weight and the composition's mean atomic mass.

Characteristic X-rays

An electron knocked out of an inner shell leaves a vacancy; an electron from a higher shell falls into it and the difference in binding energy leaves as a photon. The photon's energy therefore depends only on which two levels were involved, which is why it is characteristic of the element and not of the sample.

\[ E_{\text{line}} \;=\; E_{\text{initial}} - E_{\text{final}} \]

A Kα line fills a K vacancy from the L shell, a Kβ line fills it from the M shell, an Lα line fills an L vacancy from the M shell. The subscript separates the spin–orbit components: Kα1 comes from L3 and Kα2 from L2, and because L3 lies above L2, Kα1 is the more energetic and about twice as intense.

The absorption edge is the other side of the same physics: the energy at which an incoming photon can just eject an electron from that level. It is always slightly above every emission line that fills the level, since the line only has to reach a bound state and the edge has to reach the continuum.

Line energies rise smoothly with atomic number — Moseley's law, which is the reason the periodic table is ordered by Z rather than by atomic weight:

\[ \sqrt{\nu} \;\propto\; (Z - \sigma) \]

Energy and wavelength are the same quantity in different units:

\[ \lambda\,[\text{\AA}] \;=\; \frac{hc}{E} \;=\; \frac{12398.42}{E\,[\text{eV}]} \]

The fluorescence yield is the fraction of vacancies in a level that produce a photon rather than an Auger electron; it is small for light elements, which is why they are hard to detect by XRF. The jump ratio is the factor by which the absorption coefficient rises across the edge. Relative intensities are normalized within each series, so Kα1 against Kα2 is a real comparison and Kα1 against Lα1 is not.

Data covers Z = 1 to 98. Beyond californium no element has been produced in a quantity that allows an X-ray spectrum to be measured, so the table reports no data rather than an extrapolation. Sources for every property, with links, are listed under the periodic table itself.

Limits

  • Numerical floating-point evaluation is not symbolic algebra.
  • Use Unit Converter when dimensions or physical units matter.
  • A curve visualizes the sampled formula; it does not validate a physical model.
  • X-ray energies are tabulated values from a published compilation, not a calculation from your sample: matrix effects, chemical shifts and detector resolution are yours to account for.

Privacy

This service runs on the office intranet. Expressions are evaluated in memory for the duration of your request, nothing is stored, and no personal information is collected.
